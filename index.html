Grammar I: Simple Present - Wh- Questions and Answers

We use What and Where questions in the Simple Present to ask about jobs, daily activities, and workplaces for third-person singular (he, she, it) and plural subjects.

Wh- Word Auxiliary Subject Base Verb Answer Example
What does / do your sister / parents do? She's a vet. / They're office workers.
Where does / do his uncle / your parents work? He works in a firehouse. / They work in an office.
💡 Key Rule: With he, she, and it in affirmative answers, remember to add -s or -es to the verb (e.g., *work* ➔ *works*). For pronouns *I, you, we, they*, use the base verb. Note contractions: she's = she is, he's = he is, they're = they are.

Grammar II: Simple Present with want + infinitive

We use want to + infinitive verb to express future career aspirations, dreams, and personal desires.

Wh- Word Auxiliary Subject want Infinitive Answer Example
What do / does you / your brother want to be? I want to be a police officer. / He wants to be a scientist.
⭐ Golden Rule: Notice that with third-person singular (*he*, *she*), *want* becomes **wants**, followed directly by **to be** + noun/profession.

🧠 Critical Reflection & Logical Thinking

Discuss these open-ended questions with your classmates or teacher to connect grammar with real life:

Question 1:

Why do we use does for singular subjects (like your sister or he) and do for plural subjects (like your parents or you)? How does this rule help us speak clearly?

Question 2:

Look at the difference between "What do you do?" and "What do you want to be?". How does changing the verb phrase change the time period we are talking about (present vs. future)?

Question 3:

If someone tells you "He want to be a doctor," what grammatical error did they make according to Colegio Montano rules, and how would you politely fix it?
